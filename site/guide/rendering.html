Reference

Rendering

Outputs, aspects, covers and chapters, the quality of the blur, and the heat defaults: utility QoS, a third of the cores and an 80 °C governor.

Outputs

  • Files: the output’s extension picks the format. .mp4 (H.264, or HEVC with HEVC=1), .mov (ProRes 422 HQ), .webm, .gif.
  • Transparent: a page that leaves its background clear (the look transparent, #look=transparent, kept off the Studio’s sheet) rendered with ALPHA=1 comes out with its alpha, as .mov (ProRes 4444) or .webm (VP9 with alpha), to lay over footage in an editor. .mp4 and .gif carry no alpha and are refused. Tried on single-scene pages: a timeline film also needs an under that draws the transparent backdrop, or its cuts fill with an opaque colour.
  • Aspects: 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 or 9:16. A second aspect is a second layout, composed for its own frame, never a crop of the first.
  • Size and speed: 60 fps and 1080 pixels on the short side by default: 1920 × 1080, or 1080 × 1920 upright.
  • Covers: a still per aspect (render.mjs stills stills 0 is frame 0).
  • Chapters: a timeline of scenes carries a film of any length; chapters land in the mp4 and in a list for a YouTube description. A film that declares chapters needs three or more, the first at 0, each at least 10 seconds.

Quality

Sixteen subframes a frame are the motion blur. Each also gets a different subpixel offset on a 4×4 grid, so the blend is a 16-sample antialias as well: Chrome snaps text to whole pixels, and without it text in a slow move stepped a pixel every few frames. Rasters go through FILM.draw, which mipmaps what Chrome does not. On a real 28 s release film the worst slow-move flicker fell 37–76 %, at the same render time.

A frame that moves fast (a push, a whip) gets 48 subframes instead: at 16 its moments showed as copies about 12 px apart. A quick pass measures every frame’s speed first; on a 56 s reel 8.5 % of the frames were fast, a render about a fifth longer. The shutter (SHUTTER, 360° by default) sets how much of each frame the sixteen cover: 180° is a film camera’s, crisper, at the same cost.

Checks

A page that loses a WebGL context (Chrome keeps 16 a page, and a lost one draws nothing) fails the capture instead of delivering an empty backdrop, and each cut keeps its chunks in a folder of its own, so a square cut never deletes the master’s. Before the first frame, the soundtrack: a file without sound or an AUDIO_AT past its end stops the render, a track shorter than the film or a silent stretch is said. After the last, the file: its size, frames, length and sound are read back, and a file that does not match fails the render.

Length

  • PREVIEW=1 drafts the whole film at a sixteenth of the work.
  • FROM and TO render one chapter or one transition.
  • RESUME=1 picks up a stopped render from its chunks, refusing when the page, its scripts or the settings changed.
  • KEEP=1 keeps the chunks, so another format is an encode, not a render.

Heat

A render keeps the chip busy for minutes, and a run of test renders once took it to 91 °C. The renderer runs cool by default; these measurements chose the defaults: 8 s of reel.html (1080p60, 16 subframes) on an M5 Pro, with other work on the machine.

  • The defaults are the bold row: a third of the cores (WORKERS), the utility QoS class (QOS), the governor at 80 °C (TEMP_MAX). At the same ceiling full speed is slower and hotter: it heats past the ceiling before the governor can hold it, then spends most of the render cooling (198 s of 250).
  • A long film takes longer, not hotter. At the defaults this reel renders at about 29× real time; a film without WebGL fields or dither, faster. Draft with PREVIEW=1; leave a very long master to QOS=background, the coolest, at a third of the speed.
  • Faster, knowingly: QOS=default WORKERS=12 TEMP_MAX=86 is the old behaviour, for a short film when you ask for speed. One render at a time, always.
  • The governor reads the chip, not the render, so heat from other work holds the render too. It needs swiftc (Xcode’s command-line tools) to build thermo.swift once; without it, macOS’s own thermal state, which only acts at “serious”.
  • Spotlight: the scratch folders end in .noindex, because chunks written and deleted by the thousand kept Spotlight’s indexers busy after a render.
  • On Linux and Windows a render runs at below-normal priority, which Chrome and ffmpeg inherit, instead of a QoS class. Linux’s governor reads the CPU’s sensor (hwmon’s coretemp or k10temp, else a thermal zone); Windows has none to read, so no governor there. The renderer drives Google Chrome, else Edge on Windows or Chromium on Linux (MANOVELLA_CHROME names any), and a machine without a GPU draws the shader looks and cuts in software: slower, the same frames.

Delivery

The finished films leave the work folder for a folder of their own, and the work folder goes to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows; gio or trash-cli on Linux) once its source is safe in the product’s kit. See Making a film. deliver.mjs puts each cover through ImageOptim when it is installed, with an SSIM against the original: a real release film’s posters went from 1.0 MB to 0.13 MB at SSIM 0.9995.
