1.19.0 Big enough for a phone November 2026
  • layoutcheck.mjs fails a headline that would read under 9 pt on a phone showing the film 390 pt wide: 45 px in a 1920-wide film, 25 px in a 1080-wide vertical one, at the height it is drawn through the camera; a headline not yet begun, leaving or drawn invisible is not judged, and text inside the product’s UI drawn with TYPE.draw takes ui: true: it keeps the UI’s own rule, checked by eye on the stills. A title card on a big backdrop looks fine in a still at full size and shrinks to a line on a phone.
  • The skill says so where it talks about type, and keeps scratch files (a probe’s output, a list, a test still) in the work folder, never in /tmp.
1.18.0 A dark catalogue November 2026

1.17.1 A catalogue that fits November 2026
  • The six pictures of the catalogue (the looks, the reel, the cuts, the type sets, the headlines and the parts) are redrawn at one shape, 1600 × 1000, 16:10: each shows whole in its frame on the home page, in the README’s table and in the guide, where the wide ones used to lose their sides and the looks its last row. Each is laid out for that shape, with larger labels that stay legible at 700 pixels wide.

  • template/parts.html is every part on one sheet, a page like the others: PAGE=parts.html node render.mjs stills parts 0 makes the PNG that docs/parts.jpg is converted from.
1.17.0 A small voice, and Italian November 2026
  • Kokoro-82M is the third local voice model, beside Chatterbox and Orpheus: 330 MB where they are 3 GB and more, and quick, about a second a line once it is loaded. Its voices Heart, Bella and Michael were heard in US English, and Sara in Italian; the Studio’s voice step lists them, and each ▶ says the line through voice.mjs itself. In a story: "mlx": { "model": "kokoro", "voice": "if_sara" }.
  • A film in Italian can be told by a model: if_sara says an Italian line, and the Studio’s own sample for her is Italian. Kokoro needs its language beside the voice: it is taken from the voice’s first letter (i Italian, a American English), or set with "lang" in "mlx". No other language was heard, so none is claimed.
  • Kokoro has no sampling temperature, so there is no tone to set for it: the Studio leaves the Tone out of its reading, and a story that names one is told it changes nothing. Speed and the human touch work as for the others.
  • voice.mjs --setup kokoro also installs what Kokoro’s text needs (spaCy, misaki, the espeak-ng library, about 160 MB of wheels, pinned in voice-constraints-kokoro.txt, nothing from Homebrew), and only when Kokoro is asked for: the Python of Chatterbox and Orpheus users is not changed, and their takes came out the same to the byte before and after.
  • A Kokoro take is the same to within one 16-bit step from run to run, not to the byte, so its setup line records no SHA-256 to compare two Macs by.
1.16.2 Its own address November 2026
  • The Studio answers only to its own address: a request that asks for another host name, or that a page on another site sends it, is refused with a 403. The window, the Chrome app window and the printed link all use the address it gave, so nothing you do changes.
1.16.1 A tick in the clear November 2026

  • The Settings window says where it keeps them, Saved in … as you change them, with no full stop left alone on the last line.
  • The music step’s upload already took an .aac file; the message for a file it refuses now lists it too.
  • Claude’s instructions give the default resolution as the renderer has it: 1080 pixels on the short side, 1920 × 1080, or 1080 × 1920 upright.
1.16.0 Device frames November 2026
  • The format step has a second card, Device: what the product is shown in, whatever the format. A Mac window, a MacBook, an iMac, an iPhone, an iPad, a browser, a terminal, or Just the UI, the product’s interface on its own with no window and no device round it. Each is a tile of the stand-in product in that device, on your look, type and aspect; rest the pointer on one to watch it. Left to Claude, each format keeps the frame it was made for.
  • A release film and a tutorial have one format, and choose their device all the same: a tutorial of an iPhone app is told on an iPhone, the camera on the phone, and its subtitle says tap.
  • In a device the stand-in is laid out as that device has it, never a desktop window made small: an iPhone app with a large title, its numbers in a row, a list, and Publish under the thumb; an iPad app with a sidebar lying down and a list upright; a page in a browser with its own header and links; the app’s window on a MacBook’s desktop, or an iMac’s.
  • On an iPhone and an iPad a click is a touch: a soft disc that presses, with the look’s ripple, and the answer arrives as a banner from the top. The macOS pointer stays on the Mac.
  • Once a device is chosen, every preview after it shows the product in it: the looks, the palette, the cuts, the aspect you click in Output, the Review. The sidebar names it beside the formats, and Review has a Device row whose Edit opens its card.
  • The brief says it too, as frame, and Claude keeps to it in every storyboard. An iPhone or an iPad shows the layout your product really has there, an iOS app’s screens or a site at that width; where it has none, Claude says so before the storyboards and proposes the nearest honest frame.
  • Two new parts. PARTS.tablet: an iPad Pro in Apple’s own proportions, lying down or upright by the rect it is given, its camera on the long edge. PARTS.desktop: an iMac 24-inch on its stand, a white bezel round the display with the camera in it, a chin with no logo, in any of Apple’s seven colours or the one nearest your accent. The MacBook is redrawn beside them: an aluminium edge round the lid, a notch of the real size with its camera, a base that catches the light. See Parts.
  • The format step is a deck of two cards, like the other steps: Formats, with the three storyboards’ slots at the top of its panel while the formats scroll, and Device.
  • On the stand-in, the second click now lands on Publish now, the item it lights: it used to land on the one above it.
1.15.0 A deck of cards November 2026
  • Every step with more than one thing to choose is a deck of cards, each showing at a glance what is chosen in it: the product (a repository, a website, a release’s version, the brand colour, the name), the look (the looks, your picture, the palette), the type (the sets, any family with its weight), the motion (the headlines, the cuts, the pace and blur), the voice and the output. One card opens at a time into a panel right under its row; all closed, the step is an overview of its choices, with nothing to scroll.
  • The first card is open when a step arrives, and coming back shows the one you left open. A step’s cards stay the same whatever you choose in them: a part with nothing to set, the reading of a film with no voice say, keeps its place and says so.
  • The voice step has the line to hear and the ▶ of the chosen voice at its top, above five cards: a preset, who speaks, how it reads, the lines and subtitles, the music under the voice. A preset, a voice or a reading changed in a card is heard from there, without leaving the card.
  • Where a card holds a long list, the looks or the cuts, the row of cards stays at the top while the list scrolls. A card follows its field as you type: the film’s name, a release’s tag, the voice’s lines.
  • Review’s Edit opens the card its row is in, in every step.
  • The format step shows the three storyboards as three slots that fill in the order you click the formats, the next one marked; the ones you leave free are Claude’s. A fourth click is answered in a box of its own, in sight wherever you are in the list.
  • While a long list scrolls, its title and its filter stay under the row of cards with them, so the open card and the top of its panel remain one shape: the looks, the cuts, and the headlines, whose emphasis on one word is now always in reach there.
  • Every headline style carries every emphasis. Swap drew none, a flap board hid the marker behind its tiles, and track, wipe, split, typed and odometer never turned the word to the accent; track and odometer now stay on the stage until their mark has landed.
  • The brand’s colour: with Read it from the brand ticked the picker shows the colour guessed from the source and cannot be changed; unticked, the colour you choose is the film’s accent, and ticking the box again, or Back to the brand’s colour, brings the guess back.
  • The palette shows, for each scheme, the colours it derives from the accent, and says where a film uses them and whether your look is painted with them. Motion blur says whether it is on, and what it does, on its button and beside the preview; it now gathers a frame of the film’s own rate, twice as long for a 30 fps film.
  • With a track chosen, the ▶ beside the line plays the voice over it, mixed as the film will be at the level set in With the music, with a moment of the music alone after the line; the voice alone is one click away.
  • Any length: Custom takes a promo of 3 to 60 seconds, and a film in chapters or a tutorial of 30 seconds to 15 minutes, typed in minutes and seconds.
  • The reference says what it is: a film, a site or a style the film takes after, by link or by name. Left empty, Claude picks a real one and says what it takes from it.
  • Settings is a window of its own, laid out as a Mac’s: a sidebar with a search and one row a pane (Projects, Pictures, Music library, Work folder), each pane in grouped rows, and About Manovella at its head.
  • The previews take the shape of the first format you choose. A film of words alone, such as the social teaser, shows the looks, the colour and the cuts under its headline alone, with no window. A terminal film shows them around a terminal that types its commands, with no dashboard.
1.14.0 Settings November 2026
  • The Studio has Settings: the gear beside the logo, or Settings… (⌘,) in the app menu. Project folders, picture folders, the music library and the work folder, each chosen with the Mac’s folder picker and saved at once. See Settings.
  • The brand library is now the music library: any audio file in it, with a folder per brand optional. Delivering finished films into it is an option in Settings, off unless you turn it on, and an older settings.json keeps working.
  • The work folder, where a film’s <name>-work and its delivery go, is ~/Downloads unless you choose another; deliver.mjs follows it too.
  • Each step says where its list comes from. The product step names its folders and can take a repository from anywhere. The look step has two tabs, the looks and your pictures, with the picker first. The music step names the library’s folder.
  • The recent pictures come from ~/Pictures, or the folders you add, no longer from the Desktop and Downloads, which hold screenshots as often as backdrops.

1.12.1 A preview that did not start November 2026

1.12.0 Transparent films November 2026
  • ALPHA=1 keeps a film’s alpha, to lay it over footage in an editor: on the look transparent, which leaves the background clear, it comes out as .mov (ProRes 4444) or .webm (VP9 with alpha). .mp4 and .gif carry no alpha and are refused.
  • The look transparent draws no backdrop, grain or vignette. It is not on the Studio’s sheet: it belongs to this kind of film only.
  • Subframes are blended, and a scaled film resized, on premultiplied colour, so a blurred edge stays clean instead of fringing.
  • deliver.mjs says which files carry alpha.
1.11.0 Every headline checked November 2026
  • layoutcheck.mjs: at each moment worth a still, the headlines drawn with TYPE.draw are checked against the safe area (5 % of the short side), each other, and a contrast estimated against what surrounds them (WCAG’s ratios, 3:1 from 24 px and 4.5:1 under, as its own heuristic). Exit 1 when one fails, 2 when nothing could be checked; a headline it cannot judge (inside a cut, fading out) is listed, not passed. The limits are in its header.
  • TYPE.extent(spec, t): the block a headline rests in while it is on screen.
1.10.0 Stills side by side November 2026
  • node contact.mjs --compare A=<dir> B=<dir> [C=<dir>] out.png: two or three folders of stills in columns, one row per file name they share (the same times, named alike), for an A/B choice on one image. A still missing from a folder is named, and stills of different sizes are refused.
1.9.0 The Studio in violet; music and voice apart November 2026
  • The Studio’s window is Manovella’s violet whatever the product, with the logo in its sidebar. The product’s colour lives in the previews, and as a swatch beside its name.
  • Sound is two steps now, Which music? and Which voice?: ten steps in all.
  • Six voice presets (Promo, Launch, Narrator, Conversational, Recap, Lesson) set the voice, its tone, its tempo and its delivery at once; the Tutorial recipe starts from Lesson.
1.8.0 Manovella stands alone November 2026

Two things the product needed before anyone else can use it: its own face, and nothing of its author’s machine.

The logo

  • A 1920s movie camera, two reels on top, a violet hand crank on its side. It is the Studio’s Dock icon and the plugin’s icon.

Standalone

  • The skills, the Studio and the docs no longer name any one person’s paths, accounts, products or repositories. Every technical fact, measurement and lesson stays; examples use Acme.
  • The brand library replaces a hard-wired folder: the Studio reads MANOVELLA_BRAND, else brandLibrary in ~/Library/Application Support/manovella/settings.json, skips the names in brandLibraryIgnore, and searches for nothing. With a library, films are delivered to <library>/<Brand>/video/<name>/.
  • Remembered briefs move over from the old Film Studio folder, even when a settings.json was written first.
  • Films only on request and cool renders are Manovella’s own defaults; ImageOptim is an optional step when it is installed.
  • AGENTS.md is written for any contributor.
  • The MIT licence: LICENSE at the root and “license”: “MIT” in plugin.json. The kit’s fonts keep their own open licences.
1.7.0 The Film Studio becomes Manovella November 2026

The film engine and its Studio become a plugin of their own, with a marketplace of their own.

  • Imported: the engine, the Studio and both skills, site-promo-film and release-film, previously called Film Studio and part of another plugin.
  • Renamed: the plugin and its marketplace are manovella; the skills are manovella:site-promo-film and manovella:release-film, and the command is /manovella:studio.
  • The Studio’s window app is Manovella.app; the kit that builds it moved inside the skill, at studio/window/.
  • The FILM_STUDIO_* variables became MANOVELLA_*, and the Studio’s folders are named manovella; remembered briefs move over on the first start.
  • One version for the whole product, in plugin.json, the marketplace entry and studio.json, which the Studio’s self-test checks agree.
