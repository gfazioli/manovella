The Manovella plugin

Manovella has no account, no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reports, and nothing it does is sent to the author. Its Studio and its renders run on your computer. The few times its scripts reach the network are listed here, each one started by something you ask for.

What stays on your computer

  • The Studio is a small server that listens on 127.0.0.1 only, behind a key made for each run, and the window app opens no other page; a link inside it opens in your default browser. Its About (the logo at the top of the sidebar, or About Manovella in the app menu) shows the sponsors’ avatars, loaded from github.com when you open it, so GitHub then sees your IP address. It reads, locally, the git remote of the repositories it offers as products, the families, voices and recent pictures (in ~/Pictures and any picture folder you add in Settings) of this computer, the project folders you set, and the music library you point it at. A picture leaves its place only when you choose it, and then only as a copy in the film’s work folder.
  • A render opens the film’s page from a local file in headless Chrome and encodes with ffmpeg. The engine’s pages load nothing from the network, and the faces are the kit’s own.
  • The Studio’s music step contacts nothing. For a new track it writes a prompt you copy, and an Open Gemini link that opens Gemini’s own app in your browser; what you do there falls under Google’s terms. A track’s tempo is measured on your computer from its samples.
  • A film made from a website: when you ask for one, your agent reads that site, and your repository, as part of the work, with its own tools. No Manovella script fetches it; the Studio only turns the address you type into a link.

When it does reach the network

  • A film’s tooling. Each film’s work folder gets npm i playwright-core, which asks the npm registry for that package. It drives the Chrome you already have; no browser is downloaded.
  • The voice setup, once per machine and only if you run voice.mjs --setup. It makes a Python environment and has pip install mlx-audio and the packages pinned in voice-constraints.txt from pip’s package index, then has mlx-audio fetch each voice model you name from Hugging Face (mlx-community/chatterbox-turbo-fp16, mlx-community/orpheus-3b-0.1-ft-8bit and mlx-community/Kokoro-82M-bf16) into ~/.cache/huggingface. Asking for kokoro also installs the packages pinned in voice-constraints-kokoro.txt from pip’s index, and spaCy’s English text model, a wheel pip downloads from its release page on GitHub. On Linux and Windows the same setup has pip install PyTorch from PyTorch’s own index (download.pytorch.org) beside pip’s, with the packages pinned in voice-constraints-torch.txt and voice-constraints-large.txt, and the models are fetched from Hugging Face as PyTorch weights (hexgrad/Kokoro-82M, ResembleAI/chatterbox-turbo, unsloth/orpheus-3b-0.1-ft and hubertsiuzdak/snac_24khz). Those downloads are made by pip, mlx-audio and the models’ own packages, not by Manovella’s own code, and their own privacy statements apply. After that, a voice runs offline. brew install python@3.13 is only a command the script suggests when Python 3.13 is missing; it never runs it.
  • Another font family, only if you ask for one that is not in the kit (fonts.mjs add). It requests the family from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, then the font files its answer names), with a generic browser user-agent string and nothing else of yours.
  • Installing and updating the plugin goes through your agent (Claude Code, Codex, Copilot CLI or Gemini CLI) and GitHub, whose own policies apply.

Your requests and briefs are read by your agent’s model inside its session, under its provider’s terms (Anthropic’s for Claude Code); Manovella adds nothing to what that session sends.

What it keeps on your machine

  • The Studio’s remembered briefs (the last one for each product) and settings.json (the folders you choose in Settings), in ~/Library/Application Support/manovella/ (~/.config/manovella/ on Linux, %APPDATA%\manovella\ on Windows).
  • A cache of pictures converted for the Studio and the Chrome app window’s own profile, in ~/Library/Caches/manovella/ (~/.cache/manovella/ on Linux, %LOCALAPPDATA%\manovella\cache\ on Windows).
  • A film’s work folder, ~/Downloads/<name>-work/ (or in the work folder you choose in Settings), until you throw it away, and the delivered films where you keep them.
  • The voice environment in ~/.local/share/film-voice and the models in ~/.cache/huggingface, if you set the voices up.

This website

  • No cookies, no cross-site tracking. Its scripts set no cookie and write nothing to your browser’s storage.
  • Visit statistics. Visits are counted by Vercel Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and builds no profile of you. What we see is aggregated: pages viewed, the referring site, country, and browser or device type. Visits are grouped by a hash of the request that is discarded after 24 hours. We use it to learn which pages help.
  • Hosting. The site is static and served by Vercel. Like any host, Vercel processes your IP address and the details of each request to deliver the pages and keep them secure, and keeps those logs under its own privacy policy.
  • GitHub. The home page asks GitHub’s API for the repository’s star count and shows the sponsors’ avatars as images from github.com, so GitHub sees your IP address. Nothing is sent but the request.
  • Product Hunt. When the home page shows a Product Hunt badge, your browser loads that image from Product Hunt, which then sees your IP address.
  • YouTube. The launch film on the home page is a YouTube video. Until you press play, the page shows a picture the site serves itself and loads nothing from YouTube. Pressing play loads YouTube’s player from youtube-nocookie.com, its privacy-enhanced mode: from then on Google sees your IP address, and the player may keep data in your browser, under Google’s privacy policy.
  • The live demo on the home page is the engine’s own film, drawn in your browser from files the site serves; nothing about it leaves your browser.
  • Fonts are served by the site itself, never by a font service.
  • Email. If you write to us, we use your address and message only to answer you, and keep them no longer than the conversation needs.
  • Links. The Discord server, GitHub Sponsors and donations through Stripe lead to services of their own, and nothing is loaded from them until you click. If you use them, their own privacy policies apply.

Why, and your rights

We process the little personal data described here to run and protect the site, to learn which pages help (visit statistics, without cookies or profiles) and to answer you when you write: our legitimate interest, or the service you asked for. Vercel, GitHub and Google (when you play the film) may process it outside the European Union, including in the United States: they rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses, and we will tell you which on request.

Under the GDPR you can ask to access, correct or delete your personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to receive it in a portable format: write to privacy@undolog.com. You can also lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.
