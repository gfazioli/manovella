Guide

Making a film

From a request to a delivered film: three storyboards, stills, a draft, the masters, review rounds, other formats, delivery and cleanup.

Asking

You ask in words, in any language, and nothing renders on its own. Each request below is one message: the skill fills whatever it does not say with a default, asks what it cannot guess, and waits for you three times, at the storyboards, at the contact sheet and at the delivery.

Behind a request, Claude asks for the website or repository to base the film on (the brand, strings and screens come from its source), the duration, the format, the output, the accent, the look, the sound and where it goes. A promo is made in two versions by default, 16:9 and 9:16, each composed for its own frame; Claude asks only when you name a single aspect. The same questions are the Studio’s ten steps.

Three storyboards

Before any code, Claude shows three storyboards as tables on the music’s beat grid (time, beat, what happens), each on a different format: three storyboards are three formats, not three pacings of one idea. It recommends one, and you pick or tweak it: Storyboard B, with A’s ending. For a product that already has a film kit, the style is settled and a release film gets one storyboard.

Stills and the contact sheet

Next, one still per scene, the settled moment plus one mid-morph where a morph carries the scene, as a captioned contact sheet. Claude looks at every frame first (cramped, off-grid, illegible at that zoom, an empty hold, a ripple off the cursor’s tip) and runs clickcheck.mjs, which fails a click the viewer would not see, and layoutcheck.mjs on a page whose headlines TYPE.draw draws: the safe area, overlaps, a size that still reads on a phone, and contrast. Two looks or two takes are compared on one image with contact.mjs --compare. Then it shows you the sheet with a line per scene, and the looks sheet too while the look is still open, and waits for a go. Show me the looks sheet on the first real frame puts every preset on one sheet, to choose by eye.

A draft

A fast draft of the whole film first. PREVIEW=1 renders it at a sixteenth of the work, about 16 times faster.

The masters

On your go, the film renders, one render at a time, cool by default. The output’s extension picks the format: .mp4 (H.264, or HEVC), .mov (ProRes 422 HQ), .webm or .gif, in every aspect asked for, with a cover per aspect and, for a long film, chapter marks. Afterwards the file is read back (size, frames, length, sound) and a file that does not match fails the render instead of passing for a finished film. A loop is checked too: loopcheck compares the first frame with the last, and its PSNR must be infinite.

Review rounds

  • The headline lands on the click, not before, and a slower camera in the second scene.
  • Render 12–20 s at full quality, I want to see that transition. One range (FROM, TO).
  • Put a metronome under it, so I can hear whether the cuts land. AUDIO=click.
  • Put this track under it. AUDIO=track.mp3, brought to −14 LUFS (AUDIO_LUFS changes it, AUDIO_NORM=0 keeps the track as it is).
  • Cut it on the track’s beat. See Sound.
  • Crisper motion, like a film camera. SHUTTER=180 (or PROMO_META.shutter): the sixteen subframes cover half of each frame instead of all of it.

Other formats of the same film

  • Also as .webm for the site, and a 960 px GIF. With KEEP=1 on the master, an encode in seconds rather than a new render.
  • A ProRes copy for Final Cut. A .mov, ProRes 422 HQ.
  • A lighter mp4 under 8 MB for the newsletter. Re-encoded from the master, not rendered smaller.

Delivery and cleanup

While a film is made, everything lives in ~/Downloads/<name>-work/ (~/Downloads is the work folder unless Settings says otherwise): the source in src/, stills, sheets, drafts, the renders. A second folder holds only what gets used.

FolderHoldsLifetime
~/Downloads/<name>-work/the source in src/ (page, engine, fonts, tools, node_modules), stills, sheets, looks, drafts, the rendersuntil the film is out
<name>/, where your instructions keep finished media (else in the work folder; with Deliver finished films into it too ticked under Music library in Settings, <Brand>/video/<name>/ in it)each format and aspect of the film, a cover per aspect, a chapter list for a long filmyours, to use
  • Deliver it. deliver.mjs clones the finished files into the second folder and nothing else. It never overwrites, refuses a draft, probes every video (size, fps, frames, colour tags) and writes the chapter list a YouTube description wants. When /Applications/ImageOptim.app is installed, each cover goes through it, compared with the original by SSIM.
  • The film is out: throw the work folder away. deliver.mjs trash does a dry run first, listing anything that exists nowhere else; then, on your OK, the work folder goes to the Trash. It refuses until every source file in it is in the product’s kit (committed and on a remote branch) and every render was delivered.
  • Commit the kit to the repo, then clean up. The source goes into the product’s scripts/film/, pushed, and only then the work folder.
